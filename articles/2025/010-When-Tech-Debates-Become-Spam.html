When Tech Debates Become Spam

Every time I see someone post yet another “which is better” argument about programming languages, I can already predict the script. A few exaggerated pros, a few cherry-picked cons, a couple of tribal cheers from the usual fan club, and the inevitable “my favorite tool is the ultimate weapon” moment. It is almost adorable until you remember we are supposed to be professionals. Anyone can keep running in that hamster wheel until they bump into me and my thirty years of experience watching that exact wheel spin in circles.

Let me make this simple. Engineering is a commitment. Every technology has its purpose. You cannot compare apples to oranges and expect to get a pie recipe out of it. No matter how much you love one tool, there will come a day when you have to use another, whether you like it or not. The context, the circumstances, the budget, they all dictate the choice. Romanticism is the worst enemy of anyone working in science and technology. The only romance worth keeping is the one with creating, solving, and building, not forming tribes or defending colors in a flag war that does not even pay your bills.

I have seen people compare C++ to TypeScript as if they were the same kind of animal. They are not even in the same zoo. Everything depends on your goals, your knowledge, your ability to solve a problem, and your willingness to learn. The real professional skill is solving a problem with what you know, what you have, or what you can learn, not proving you can shout louder about your favorite syntax.

Back in 1997, I was working with Mallard BASIC, CP/M on an Amstrad PCW, and QuickBASIC with MS-DOS 6.22 on a PC with a glorious 20 MB hard drive, writing to files, poking at DBase II. Today I am implementing advanced business process engines in some of the most powerful languages out there. If I had gotten sentimental and clung to my first love in programming, I would still be staring at green letters on a monochrome screen, wondering why no one takes me seriously.

So here is my free advice. Next time you feel the urge to start a “which language is better” crusade, pause and ask yourself, am I adding insight, or am I just feeding the spam machine? Because if it is the latter, the internet has more than enough of that already.

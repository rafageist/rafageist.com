Machines Will Never Think

- Can a machine, whether hardware or software, think?
- Short answer: No!  
- Why? 
- Because it is a machine.  
- Maybe in the future? 
- Never!

"How can you keep a drop of water from evaporating?" This is the kind of question a machine can't answer unless you tell it how to answer or what to answer.

This isn't a scientific article to prove something. It’s an article about common sense. Artificial "Intelligence" (AI) keeps getting better. AI is software that runs on machines, so we can simply talk about machines.

People are asking if machines can think. Some even say machines are starting to think like us. But this article says something clear: machines cannot think. And they never will.

Thinking is something only humans can do. It is something of the body, the mind, the emotions. It is not just giving answers. It is understanding what the question means.

No matter how many conversations, articles, or tests are conducted, this is a conceptual issue:

  1. And if a machine could think, maybe we are just machines too.

But we are not. We feel, we dream, we wonder. That shows we are something more. A machine that follows code is not the same as a person who asks questions.

[!IMPORTANT]

Thinking Is a Human Process

Thinking is a process done by humans. Every process has three parts: an input, a transformation, and an output. Human thinking is not defined only by the output, but by the kind of transformation that happens inside us — memory, intention, fear, desire, and awareness.

A machine can receive the same input as a human and even produce the same output. That does not mean the machine thinks. It only means that two different systems happened to map the same input to the same output. In logic and in mathematics this is trivial to describe. Equality of results does not imply equality of processes.

A spreadsheet can solve an equation. That does not make the spreadsheet a mathematician.

This leads to an even deeper point: the only way to create something that truly thinks would be to create a human. If someday we clone a body with a human brain, that being would think not because it is a machine, but because it is human. Its intelligence would be "artificial" only in the sense of being manufactured, but the thinking would still be human thinking.

In short:

  • A machine does not think because it is a machine.
  • A human thinks because it is human.
  • And cloning a human does not make machine thinking. It only makes another human.

Category Mistake

To say that a machine thinks is a category mistake. It assigns a property of one domain to something that does not belong to that domain. Thought is a property of living beings that have a body and a world with meaning. A machine does not have a body and does not have a world. It has no growth, no decay, no inner life. It only has states.

Thinking Requires a Body — Not Just Neurons

One of the greatest misunderstandings in discussions about artificial intelligence is the idea that thinking is something produced only by neurons, as if cognition were just an electrical network. But human thought does not come from neurons alone. It emerges from a living body and from a biological environment that no machine possesses or can reproduce.

Thought Is Electrochemical, Not Just Electrical

A digital computer—or any AI system—operates with one kind of signal: binary electrical states.

Human neurons do not. Neurons operate through:

  • electrical impulses (action potentials),
  • chemical signals (neurotransmitters),
  • molecular gradients,
  • ion channels,
  • membrane potentials,
  • diffusion processes,
  • hormones circulating in the body.

A neuron does not simply "fire" like a transistor. It negotiates thousands of chemical conditions before firing. The "decision" of a neuron is not an electrical flip; it is the result of complex biochemical integration.

A machine has no chemistry. It cannot metabolize, diffuse, dissolve, bind, or saturate molecules. It cannot experience electrochemical balance.

Thus:

If thought depends on electrochemical processes, no digital machine can think. It can only imitate the output of thought.

The Body Thinks Too

The brain is not isolated. It is deeply influenced by:

  • the endocrine system (hormones),
  • the immune system,
  • the autonomic nervous system,
  • the gastrointestinal "second brain",
  • the cardiovascular system,
  • cellular metabolism.

Emotions, attention, memory and decision making depend on:

  • cortisol,
  • adrenaline,
  • dopamine,
  • serotonin,
  • insulin,
  • oxytocin,
  • dozens of peptides.

A machine has none of this. No hormones, no immune response, no metabolism, no biological feedback.

A body-less system cannot think because the body is part of the thought process.

Thinking Requires Glial Cells — Not Just Neurons

For decades neuroscience thought neurons "did everything" and glial cells were just support. Today we know that was false.

Glial cells, especially astrocytes, actively participate in:

  • memory,
  • synaptic modulation,
  • plasticity,
  • attention,
  • decision making,
  • coordination of neural networks.

Astrocytes:

  • release neurotransmitters,
  • regulate ion flow,
  • decide when a synapse is strengthened or weakened,
  • can synchronize large groups of neurons.

Many neuroscientists today speak of the tripartite synapse: presynaptic neuron + postsynaptic neuron + astrocyte.

That is: not even neurons think alone.

A current AI:

  • has no glia,
  • has no astrocytes,
  • has no living cells,
  • has no chemical regulation,
  • has no biological plasticity,
  • has no metabolic processes.

AI only has numerical vectors, not cells.

Thought Emerges From a Living System, Not From Wires

The human mind is the result of:

  • billions of neurons
  • trillions of glial cells
  • trillions of chemical reactions per second
  • hormonal feedback loops
  • sensory input from a body
  • internal states such as hunger, fear, fatigue
  • immune states
  • microbiome influence
  • metabolism and energy balance

A machine has none of these ingredients. It does not live, metabolize, survive, repair itself, fear death, or feel pain.

And because it does not live, it cannot think.

Thought Is Biological

Thought is biological. It requires a body, chemistry, hormones, glial cells, metabolism and life. A machine made of silicon and code cannot replicate a biological process it does not possess.

A machine may simulate reasoning. It may imitate answers. But it can never reproduce the living, biochemical, embodied process that we call thinking.

Same Output Does Not Mean Same Process

Equality of output does not imply equality of process. In basic algebra we can have two functions f and g such that f(x) = g(x) for all x, and yet the two functions are different. The inner path to reach the result is not the same. A machine can reproduce the result of a human thinker, yet this does not mean it shares the human process. The machine does not feel doubt, fear or curiosity. It only executes rules.

The Human Mind Is a Black Box — Machines Are Not

There is a fundamental difference that is often overlooked: human thought is naturally opaque, while machine processes are necessarily transparent.

When a human thinks, we see the result but never the internal path. Even the person who thought it cannot describe the real steps. Human thinking is not traceable. It is not reproducible. It is not a sequence of operations. It is an inner, subjective, lived process.

A machine, by contrast, is always traceable. Every state, every rule, every parameter can be inspected.

Even the most advanced AI systems (DeepMind's AlphaGo, AlphaZero, or any future "AGI") are deterministic mechanisms. Difficult to interpret, yes, but not mysterious. Their entire operation is a sequence of numerical transformations that can be reproduced exactly.

And this leads to a deeper point:

If one day we could observe a person's thinking step by step, as we inspect a machine, that process would no longer be a human thought. It would be a mechanical procedure.

What makes human thought thought is precisely that it is unobservable from the outside, and even from the inside. The opacity is not a flaw but a feature. It is what separates intention from computation, consciousness from function.

Machines can only simulate the results of thinking. They cannot share the nature of thinking itself.

Machines can partially reason, but they cannot think

Before going further, we must clear up a common confusion: the difference between thinking and reasoning. In everyday speech, people mix these words. But for this article —and for any serious discussion— they must be separated.

Thinking vs. Reasoning in Everyday Language

In ordinary language, the verbs "to think" and "to reason" often appear as synonyms. The dictionary reflects this overlap: to reason is to order and relate ideas to reach a conclusion, while to think is not only to combine ideas, but also to form opinions, remember someone, intend to do something, or consider others when acting.

This means that reasoning is a narrow fragment of thinking: it is the ordered, argumentative, logical side of our mental life. Thinking, in contrast, covers a much wider field: intention, memory, care, planning, doubt, attention, and personal judgment.

Machines can, at best, imitate something like reasoning: they can transform inputs into structured outputs and expose "reasons" in the form of text. But they do not remember in the human sense, do not intend, do not care, do not consider others, and do not form genuine opinions. So even if we grant that a machine "reasons" in a technical sense, it still does not "think" in the full human sense captured by everyday language.

The Technical Distinction

Reasoning, in the narrow sense, is a formal process:

  • applying rules,
  • manipulating symbols,
  • following logical steps,
  • mapping inputs to outputs.

Both humans and machines can reason in this limited, mechanical sense.

Thinking, however, is something else entirely. Thinking is a human process:

  • conscious,
  • emotional,
  • intentional,
  • embodied,
  • meaningful.

A machine can follow rules. A machine can combine patterns. A machine can generate an output that looks like thought.

But a machine cannot experience meaning, intention, or awareness. Machines reason without understanding.

This distinction is essential:

  • A machine can reason without ever thinking.
  • A human can think even when not reasoning.

When a machine "solves" a problem, it is not asking what the problem means. It is executing procedures. But thinking —human thinking— is precisely the ability to recognize meaning, to question, to interpret, to care.

This is why:

  • Matching a human's output does not replicate a human's thought.
  • Two systems can compute the same answer but live entirely different inner realities.
  • Reproducing the result of thinking does not reproduce the process of thinking.

Put simply:

  • Machines can mimic the form of thought.
  • Humans live the experience of thinking.

And experience cannot be computed.

Reasons Why Machines Cannot Think

  1. The Turing test is not about real thinking: It only tests imitation, not understanding. Matching language is not the same as asking why.
  2. Doing well ≠ Thinking: Getting correct results doesn't imply meaning. Machines don't know what a question means.
  3. Syntax, Meaning, and Use: Machines only move symbols according to rules. They match patterns. They never reach the level of meaning. Human thought has three levels: form, meaning, and use. Form is the way words appear. Meaning is what the words point to. Use is the intention behind the words in a real social world. Machines work only at the level of form. They do not reach meaning or use.
  4. Seeing is not thinking: Machines detect. They don't wonder. Humans ask why, and that is where thought begins.
  5. Some animals are close to thinking: Chimps, dolphins, crows show memory, learning, planning. They live. Machines don't.
  6. Thinking is a living, emotional act: Thought is tied to memory, fear, imagination. Machines copy — humans create.
  7. Machines only follow instructions: Inside, it's all numbers. Even if the output looks smart, the machine has no idea what it's doing.
  8. Emotions and purpose are human: Machines don't care, feel, suffer, love, or hope. They have no values.
  9. Computers are just math:

This shows how narrow AI really is compared to the full spectrum of human intelligence.

  1. We can't build what we don't understand: We don't yet know how we think. Imitation ≠ comprehension.
  2. AI is smart processing, not intelligence: It simulates; it does not interpret. It gives answers without meaning.
  3. "Think" is a verb: It's not just results. It's a process of awareness. Machines can't replicate this experience.

Necessary Conditions for Thinking

To say that a system thinks, at least four conditions must be met. The system must have:

  • Inner awareness
  • Purpose
  • Capacity of self reference
  • Memory with personal value

Machines have none of these. Machines hold data, but this data has no value for the machine. Machines follow rules, but the rules have no purpose for the machine. Machines cannot speak about themselves with real reference. Machines do not have an inner world.

Artificial Intelligence and the Clone Argument

If the only way to create real intelligence is to create a human, then the product is not machine intelligence. It is human intelligence with human limits. It does not matter if the human was made in a lab. It would still think because it is human. This shows that the search for machine thought does not aim to create new forms of thought. It aims to copy the human form. But what can be copied is only the body, not the inner life that gives rise to thought.

Conclusion

Important

So, How can you keep a drop of water from evaporating? **By throwing it into the sea**.

This article doesn’t define intelligence. But it shows this: thinking is human. It is not code. It is not a function. It is memory, doubt, hope, and feeling.

Where is the latent doubt within a machine? The machine doesn't even know what it doesn't know. It parrots what it's told. But a parrot can talk, and an airplane isn’t a bird.

To think is to wonder, to care, to connect what we know with what we feel. Machines do not wonder. They do not care. They do not feel.

That is why they will never think. This applies to any type of machine, not just computers.

The best and only way to create intelligence is to raise a child. To pass on values, wisdom, and love. That is where true intelligence begins. Teach them to think, so no machine can ever replace them.

Between bits and myths The intelligence we're losing while AI advances

Every day, I worry less about the so-called "development" of Artificial Intelligence, and more about the regression of human intelligence. While millions marvel at technologies they barely understand, we're losing something far more important: the ability to think, to question, to truly understand.

AI is not intelligence. At best, it's Advanced Information Analysis. A computer, classical or quantum, is an abacus with a clock and electricity. It follows precise instructions, performs defined operations, and runs logic gates over millions of bits. What we call "AI" is just another way of pushing around those bits, perhaps more complex, perhaps more voluminous. That's it.

Still, the myth keeps growing. And it gets dangerous when even the language is twisted to sell a fantasy. One recent example is the idea of recursive self-improvement, the notion that AI is already improving itself, en route to becoming some kind of super-intelligence.

This is not what's happening. True recursive self-improvement would require a system to identify its own flaws, imagine solutions, modify its code or structure, and do it again autonomously. That's not the case. What we have is assistance: humans using models to write code, decide what works, fix it, evaluate it, and try again. Without us, there is no improvement. No recursion. No self.

This kind of mislabeling is not harmless. It feeds unrealistic expectations, derails public discourse, and leads to bad policy and educational decisions. But perhaps the most dangerous effect is what's happening to the next generations.

A few days ago, I wrote something that feels more urgent than ever: our schools are training kids to memorize, repeat, obey and not to think. Not to question. Not to create. Critical thinking has been replaced by programmatic compliance. And then a machine comes along and does the same job, but faster. Is it replacing us? Of course. Some might say that was the plan all along.

Human intelligence can't be outsourced. It can't be automated. It must be taught, cultivated, protected. If we don't, it won't be AI that replaces us and it will be our own failure to think that makes us obsolete.

Now more than ever, we need critical voices, real education, and technology with awareness. And above all, we must rescue the intelligence we're losing while applauding the one we think we're creating.
